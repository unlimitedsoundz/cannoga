# Editorial Content

## Purpose
The Editorial Content skill ensures all written copy within application interfaces, landing pages, and components sounds authored, precise, factual, and institutionally grounded. It eliminates generic AI marketing clichés, empty buzzwords, and vague promotional fillers.

## When to Use
Apply this skill when drafting interface copy, headline titles, hero section leads, empty state messages, button labels, modal descriptions, or feature summaries.

## Core Principles
1. **Factual Specificity**: Replace empty claims with concrete data, specific program names, exact requirements, and verified dates.
2. **Institutional Voice**: Write in a composed, clear, authoritative tone appropriate for an established organization (e.g., academic, public sector, clinical, financial).
3. **No AI Marketing Clichés**: Eliminate corporate filler and generic buzzwords that populate automated AI copy templates.
4. **Fact Integrity**: Never invent facts, fake statistics, non-existent rankings, or unverified accreditation claims.

## Rules
1. **Banned Slogan Catalog**: Strictly prohibit the following generic AI marketing phrases:
   - *"Empowering the next generation of..."*
   - *"Unlock your potential with..."*
   - *"Transform your future..."*
   - *"World-class education / experience..."*
   - *"Discover a world of possibilities..."*
   - *"Seamlessly revolutionize your workflow..."*
   - *"Next-gen solution for modern..."*
   - *"Take your X to the next level..."*
2. **Headline Specificity Formula**: Headlines must clearly state *what* the page/section is, *who* it serves, or *what actionable information* it contains.
   - *Bad (AI Cliché)*: "Empowering Tomorrow's Leaders Today"
   - *Good (Authored)*: "Department of Civil Engineering & Infrastructure Studies"
3. **Actionable Button Labels**: Buttons must state specific actions (*"Download Course Syllabus (PDF)"*, *"Submit Registration Form"*, *"View Degree Requirements"*) rather than vague imperatives (*"Get Started"*, *"Learn More"*, *"Click Here"*).
4. **Concise Form Labels & Hints**: Provide clear, unambiguous instructions for inputs (e.g., *"Student Identification Number (9 digits)"* instead of *"Enter your ID"*).

## Do
- **Use Domain Terminology**: Incorporate precise operational terms (e.g., *Matriculation, Prerequisite, Credit Load, Office of the Registrar, Academic Standing, Term GPA*).
- **Vary Sentence Lengths**: Mix short, crisp factual statements with structured descriptive sentences to maintain natural cadence.
- **Provide Useful Descriptions**: Explain the exact purpose of a page, form, or document in 1-2 clear sentences.

## Don't
- **Don't** fill hero sections with hyperbole ("The world's premier institution for...").
- **Don't** use empty superlative adjectives ("unparalleled", "groundbreaking", "synergistic", "cutting-edge").
- **Don't** invent fake statistics ("99.9% Student Satisfaction Guaranteed!").
- **Don't** write long, repetitive marketing body copy where a structured table or bulleted list is clearer.

## Implementation Guidance
- **Comparison of AI Copy vs. Authored Institutional Copy**:

  | Element | Generic AI Copy (Banned) | Authored Institutional Copy (Required) |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | **Hero Lead** | "Unlock endless opportunities and empower your career with our world-class computer science programs." | "Undergraduate and graduate degree tracks in Computer Science, offering specialization in Distributed Systems, Cybersecurity, and Software Engineering." |
  | **Card Subtitle** | "Experience seamless learning designed to transform your future." | "Prerequisite coursework covers Data Structures (CS-201), Discrete Mathematics (MATH-150), and Systems Programming (CS-205)." |
  | **CTA Button** | "Get Started Now" | "View 2026 Academic Calendar & Deadlines" |
  | **Empty State** | "Nothing here yet! Start your journey today!" | "No active course enrollments found for the selected academic term." |

## Review Checklist
- [ ] Are all banned AI slogans ("Empowering...", "Unlock your potential...") completely removed?
- [ ] Is all copy factual, grounded, and specific to the organization's domain?
- [ ] Are button labels explicit about their action rather than generic ("Get Started")?
- [ ] Are all claims, stats, and dates accurate and un-invented?
- [ ] Does the overall voice sound written by a professional human editor for an established institution?
